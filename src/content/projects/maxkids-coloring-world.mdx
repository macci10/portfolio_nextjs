An internal Maxxsol product taken end to end, from architecture through iOS and
Android release, working alongside a junior developer. The drawing engine is the
product — everything else exists to keep it fed with content and to keep the
monetisation from getting in a child's way.

Flood fill is where a coloring app is won or lost. A naive recursive fill on a
tablet-sized canvas either blows the stack or drops frames long enough that a
four-year-old taps again and fills the wrong region. The engine uses a
queue-based fill instead, which keeps the work bounded and the frame budget
intact. On top of it sit SVG textured brush strokes, pinch-to-zoom to 5x
through `InteractiveViewer`, and a Magic Rainbow mode that shifts colour as the
stroke travels.

Children do not finish drawings in one sitting, so the pixel buffer is persisted
to Hive with auto-save, and a session resumes exactly where it stopped.

The content pipeline pulls zip-archived packs from Firebase Storage and unpacks
them locally. The awkward part was vector dimensions: SVGs authored at different
canvas sizes render at different scales across devices, so the pipeline
auto-patches them on ingest to normalise rendering. A standalone Python toolkit
(`svg_optimizer`, `png_to_svg`, `trim_asset`, `find_svg_bbox`) does the tracing,
scaling, and bounding-box normalisation before art ever reaches a pack.

Monetisation is banner, interstitial, and rewarded ads — but export, share, and
PDF printing all sit behind an arithmetic parental gate. Anything that sends an
image off the device is a decision an adult should make.
