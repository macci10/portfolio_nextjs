A proof of concept for reading colorimetric test strips reliably under
uncontrolled lighting. The interesting problem isn't reading colour — it's that
the phone camera never gives you the colour that's actually there.

Auto white balance, auto exposure, the colour temperature of whatever light the
user happens to be standing under, and the phone's own tone curve all sit
between the pad and the pixel. Two photos of the same strip taken a metre apart
disagree. So the pipeline has to treat the captured RGB as a corrupted signal
and recover the reading from it, rather than trusting it.

The work splits in two. **Detection** validates the capture (a Laplacian
variance blur check rejects a shot that isn't sharp enough to sample), maps
screen coordinates through the on-screen guide, and localises each pad's region
of interest. **Interpretation** then calibrates, converts sRGB to CIELAB,
compares against reference swatches, and interpolates between them.

Two decisions carried most of the accuracy. The first was moving from CIE76 to
**CIEDE2000** for colour distance — CIE76 is a plain Euclidean distance in
CIELAB and it systematically misjudges the blue and yellow regions the pH and
alkalinity pads live in. The second was **per-pad local white balance**: the
strip has white gutters printed between the pads, so each pad gets its own Von
Kries channel scaling derived from the white immediately next to it, rather than
one global correction for the whole photo. Specular highlights — pixels above
240 luminance, usually a reflection off the wet strip — are dropped before
sampling.

The strip layout is configuration, not code, so the same pipeline reads the
AquaChek 4-parameter and 7-parameter strips.
